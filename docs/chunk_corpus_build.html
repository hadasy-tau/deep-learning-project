Preprocessing build record · 9 September 2026

One speaker per chunk: a 30-second corpus from the Knesset committees

src/preprocessing/preprocessing.py turns the committees speaker index into audio — chunks of at most 30 seconds, each holding exactly one speaker, over the whole corpus. It has now been built: 410 shards, 274.5 GB of lossless FLAC, all 10,905 sessions, at Hadasy/knesset-committees-chunks.

Why

The plenum corpus can't measure the plenum model

ivrit-ai/whisper-large-v3 was fine-tuned on knesset-plenums-whisper-training, about 4,700 hours of plenum audio, interleaved 0.15:0.8:0.05 with crowd-transcribed and crowd-recital data. Scoring that model on VoxKnesset — also derived from the plenums — measures train-on-test as much as it measures adaptation. The committees corpus is not in that mixture, so it is genuinely held out for both openai/whisper-large-v3 and the ivrit.ai fine-tune.

The identity work was already done. Dolevabudi/knesset-committees-speakers resolves every aligned segment to an official Knesset PersonID with demographics — but it publishes an index only. Its filename column names files that do not exist, and nothing in the repository could turn those names into audio. This module is that missing step.

It is deliberately blind to what consumes it: no speaker selection, no hours budget, no train/dev/test split, no column named for a downstream notebook. Those are filters over a corpus, not properties of one.

The rule

Windows stop at the speaker change

The chunker follows ivrit.ai's own create_dataset.py — the same 30-second packing, the same 0.3 s gap merging, the same restart-on-overflow semantics, so windows tile a speaker's turn back to back with no gaps and no overlap. One rule is added: a window may never cross a turn boundary.

Their slicer packs whatever segments fall inside the next 30 seconds regardless of who is speaking. That is correct for a training corpus and fatal here — a chunk containing two people has no well-defined speaker_id, and every per-speaker number computed from it is contaminated.

Same segments, two slicersseconds

A turn is defined over every row, not the assigned ones

A turn is a maximal run of one speaker — same session, same local_speaker_id, same speaker_id — and it has to be derived over the whole index, not over the rows that resolved to a person. Two ways of getting this wrong were caught by measurement rather than by reasoning:

  • Deriving runs from the assigned subset alone. An unresolved guest speaking between two of a member's segments ends that member's turn; filtering first merges straight across them. It reported a mean turn span of 67.4 s against a true 15.8 s, and would have produced chunks containing the guest.
  • Not breaking the run when speaker_id changes. 49,679 turns contain a mix of assigned and unassigned segments from the same local speaker, because the identity pass labels per word and only assigns where the protocol's gold speaker, the name and the seat all agree — a name_disagree segment stays unresolved mid-turn. Tiling across that gap puts speech into a chunk whose attribution was deliberately refused, with no reference text covering it. Breaking the run there excludes 205 hours of exactly that audio.

With both fixed, no run in the corpus holds more than one speaker — asserted over all 936,406 of them, not sampled.

A chunk ends at its last segment, not at seek + 30

ivrit.ai always grab a full window of audio. Here that would read past the turn boundary into the next speaker, so the chunk's audio stops at the last segment packed into it.

Transfer

One session in flight, then deleted

There is no bulk download. The build walks sessions one at a time: stream that session's audio.m4a, verify the transfer completed, decode it once to 16 kHz mono PCM, cut and FLAC-encode its chunks, write that session's parquet part, delete the m4a.

Peak transient disk is therefore three files, not a corpus — the median recording is 58 MB and the largest 524 MB. The 672.7 GB figure below is cumulative bytes moved across the whole run; it was never resident.

Status

Built, verified, and two gates still open

The corpus is complete: 10,905 of 10,905 sessions, 410 shards with no gaps and no duplicate indices, verified three independent ways — the repo listing, the uploaded-session ledger, and the run log's own per-shard counts, which agree exactly.

What went wrong, and what it cost

A Hub outage killed the run at 88%. HuggingFace returned 503 on the commit endpoint after 29 hours. That was their failure, but the consequence was a gap in this code: the download path retried with jittered backoff and the upload path had none, so one transient error was fatal. Uploads now retry the same way, and the fix proved necessary within a minute — re-flushing the stranded shard hit another 503 and succeeded on the second attempt. Nothing was lost: the 36 part files stranded mid-upload were complete, and shipping them beat rebuilding them.

Seven sessions of 10,905 were too long to decode whole. A 12 h recording is 1.35 GB of PCM in one worker, doubled transiently by ffmpeg's captured stdout, against ~4 GB free — so MAX_SESSION_S refused them rather than risking an OOM at hour thirty. They were recovered afterwards by a second decoder that asks ffmpeg for one turn's span at a time, bounding memory by the longest turn instead of the recording: 3,094 chunks across 55 speakers, and all thirteen chunk invariants hold on them.

What is still not verified

Speaker attribution comes from the written protocol, which records who held the floor rather than who was audible. The ear check was spot-checked on a handful of clips, not swept across all 35. And the ECAPA speaker-embedding check — the only test that compares a chunk against its speaker's voice rather than against the protocol — needs a GPU and has never been run. Until it does, this corpus's speaker labels are as good as the protocol, which is not the same as being as good as the tape.

  1. Install ffmpeg and soundfile — done
    ffmpeg 9.0.1 (Gyan build)  ·  soundfile 0.14.0 / libsndfile 1.2.2
    soundfile is not optional in practice: without it every chunk shells out to ffmpeg, which is 1.19M subprocess spawns on a full build. Note that winget's PATH change needs a fresh terminal, or set $FFMPEG to the binary.
  2. Corpus self-checks — passing
    python src/preprocessing/preprocessing.py --fetch
    The flag pulls the 520 MB index; without it the corpus checks skip, so a routine run never starts a download nobody asked for.
  3. Pilot on twenty sessions — passed
    1,333 runs → 1,729 chunks, 5.77 h, in 4.7 min at 4 workers
    All thirteen chunk invariants hold, including that two speakers never overlap in time within a session. Both predicted edge cases appeared and were handled: one m4a shorter than its alignment (2 chunks dropped past EOF) and one carrying 3 h of audio for a single chunk.
  4. Listen — spot-checked, not swept
    ear check page, 35 clips across the 20 pilot sessions
    A few clips were played and matched their text. That is real evidence, but about the sessions that happened to be heard: a time-origin offset is a per-session fault, so the ones not listened to are not covered.
  5. Build and ship — done
    python src/preprocessing/run_build.py  ·  410 shards, 274.5 GB, 36.6 h
    Streamed straight to the Hub, one shard at a time, so local disk never held more than a shard plus the sessions in flight.
  6. Recover the oversized sessions — done
    python src/preprocessing/recover_long.py  ·  7 sessions, 3,094 chunks, shard 00409
    Per-turn ranged decode, so memory follows the longest turn rather than the recording.

    ECAPA centroids per speaker, flag outliers
    Needs a GPU. The one check that tests the labels against the voice rather than against the protocol — and the one failure mode both text sources share.
Open

What this cannot check

Speaker attribution comes from the written protocol, which records who held the floor — not who was audible. Committee sittings have heavy cross-talk, and a chunk labelled for one member may carry another voice over it. The per-chunk quality score catches some of that, since overlap degrades alignment confidence, but not all of it.

The real gate is Step 4.2 of the identity plan — the ECAPA speaker-embedding check that compares each chunk against its speaker's centroid. It needs a GPU and has never been run. Until it does, the corpus's precision figure of 1.000 means against the protocol, not against the voice on the tape. It should run on the finished corpus.

One smaller item, for whoever does the subgroup analysis: three place_of_birth values in this corpus — אוסטריה, דנמרק and האימפריה האוסטרו־הונגרית, one speaker each — are not covered by the existing origin mapping, and unmapped values become Unknown and drop out silently.
