Personalized Hebrew ASR

Does adapting an ASR model to one speaker help, and for whom? The corpus is ~15,500 h of Knesset committee audio with the official protocol force-aligned to it — recordings no Hebrew Whisper checkpoint has trained on. Seven stages turn that into one answer per speaker, then an adapter for the speakers the general Hebrew model failed, then a verdict on it.

5 · Choose who to adapt

gain = WERA − WERB

Low gain is the signal: the Hebrew fine-tune did nothing for this voice, so that is where personalization has headroom. High-gain speakers are already served.

1 adapterper cell

6 · Fine-tune them

src/training/train.py

More of the chosen speaker's audio → session-disjoint splits, latest sessions held out → one adapter per cell (speaker, arm, site, method, budget, rank, lr, seed).

a verdictper speaker

7 · Evaluate the adapters

src/evaluation/evaluate.py

Base and tuned scored down the identical path, on the speaker's held-out sessions, with a paired bootstrap over chunks. Did adaptation help, where did it act, and how many minutes did it take?

Why 1 h per speaker. The per-speaker CI half-width reaches ~0.04 WER at 45 min — enough to rank by gain. 230 h cost under $20 and under two hours of wall time; the full 3,840 h would run ~$104 (A) + ~$150 (B) and buy stage 5 nothing.

One id, one inference path

Two decisions hold the whole pipeline together. A speaker's identity is resolved once, to an id the Knesset already assigns; and the audio that id selects goes through one inference path, called twice — wide and shallow to rank every speaker, then deep on the few that are chosen.

  • The id is not ours. PersonID is the Knesset's own key, read from the parliament's public ODATA service, which also supplies each member's name variants and service dates. It is the same id space as HaifaCLGroup/KnessetCorpus, so the roster, the demographics and the protocols all join directly — and the session key joins the same way: ivrit's session_id is ODATA's CommitteeSessionID.
  • Accuracy over coverage. A match must be exact and must fall inside the person's service dates. A name that cannot be resolved is dropped, not guessed: a wrong id is far worse than no id.
  • The id is also the selector, which is what makes screening and personalization the same program rather than two. Only the filter changes: 1 h of every speaker, or everything left of one chosen speaker.
  • One seam is unavoidable. A trained adapter is not served by the providers, so stage 7 runs locally. Comparisons therefore stay inside one engine — A vs B over the providers, base vs tuned locally — while the scoring is shared: normalize_he from src/common.py, error counts not rates, micro-aggregated.

The caveat that outlives every stage. Speaker labels are verified against the protocol, not against the voice. The protocol records who held the floor, not who was audible, and committee cross-talk is heavy. If one speaker's WER looks anomalous, suspect the labels before the model.
